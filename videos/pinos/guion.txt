I just cut down the oldest pine in the valley. And up on the mountain, something stood up. In Hollowpine, you never cut a living tree. Cut one, and the Watcher comes down for you. Our village sits in a valley of pines, buried in snow half the year. We only burn what falls. Branches, pinecones, old fences. Every morning, the kids drag sleds into the woods and pick up sticks. And every night, it watches us from the tree line. All you can see are its antlers. Wider than the river. And two pale eyes, floating between the trunks. Stare back too long, and your lantern goes out. Grandma Ilse says it was here before the first cabin. When she was a girl, a woodcutter named Brask cut one pine for a new roof. That night, the whole forest went quiet. Then the ground started shaking, one step at a time. It walked out of the trees, taller than the church. It stepped on Brask's cabin like it was a pinecone. Then it walked back into the dark. Brask was never found. His stump is still in the square. Nobody sits on it. We carve its antlers on every door, so it knows we remember. This winter is the worst in a hundred years. The snow buried every fallen branch. We burned our fences. Then our chairs. Last night, my little sister Mila stopped shivering. Grandma says that's the bad kind of cold. Our fire was out. Mila's lips were blue. So I took Brask's old axe from the shed. I walked to the oldest pine. Hollow and gray, right at the tree line. I swung until my hands bled. It fell. And inside the hollow trunk, something moved. A little deer, made of bark and moss, curled up and shaking. A baby. Its baby. Up on the mountain, the Watcher stood up. I picked up the baby and ran home with the wood. I lit the fire. I put the baby next to Mila. The ground shook. The cups fell off the shelf. Its eye filled our whole window. One antler tore our roof open. Snow fell on the bed. Then it saw its baby, warm, next to my sister. It stopped. It lay down around our cabin, like a wall of fur. And all night, its breath kept us warm. In the morning, Mila was laughing. Her cheeks were pink again. The baby was gone, and the forest was quiet. But outside our door was a pile of fallen branches, taller than our roof. My whole life, the Watcher watched us. Last night, it watched over us.
