We live on the back of a turtle. Dig deeper than one shovel, and it dives. And the whole town goes down with it. It walks the shallow sea, one step every hour. Every step rattles every cup in Shellbrook. We plant carrots in one shovel of dirt. We bury our goldfish in one shovel of dirt. Mayor Quill checks every hole with a yellow ruler. The sign in the square says, one shovel, no more. Every house has a ruler hanging by the door. Kids can't own a shovel until they turn twelve. I got mine last spring. From the edge of town, you can see its head. It's bigger than the church. Barnacles grow on its eyelids. Every morning, it breathes out a cloud warm enough to dry our laundry. At night I lie on the shell and feel its heart. Slow, like a drum underwater. Sometimes it hums, and the windows hum back. When I was six, Pim the baker wanted a cellar. Two shovels deep. Just two. The ground went cold under our feet. The turtle stopped walking. Then the whole town tilted. Seawater poured down the streets. The bakery slid off the edge and sank, bread still warm. Pim filled the hole with his bare hands. The turtle rose again, slowly, like nothing happened. Pim never baked again. He sells rulers now. Mayor Quill bought the first one. But I noticed something that day. When it dove, it dove away from the hole. Nobody else saw it. Everyone else was holding on. This morning, the turtle turned. Away from the shore. Toward the deep water. Old Garrow climbed onto its head with his telescope. He came down very quiet. He says it's old. It's going home. Turtles go back to where they were born. And home is at the bottom. With every step, the water climbs higher on the shell. The fish market goes under. Then the school. The water reaches the steps of the church. Mayor Quill tells everyone to pray. Everyone runs uphill, to the top of the shell. Old Garrow stays at the bell, ringing and ringing. I run the other way, with my shovel. To the very edge, on the deep water side. Mayor Quill yells my name. Then everybody yells my name. One shovel. Two. The ground goes cold. Three. I hit something hard. Something alive. Warm, under the dirt, like skin. The turtle flinches. And slowly, it turns away from the hole. Back toward the shore. The water slides off the streets like a long sigh. My whole life, one shovel kept us safe. Today, three shovels brought us home.
